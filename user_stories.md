### User stories

1. Skyler works in natural ingredient discovery at a biotechnology company. They want to explore new phenylprepanoids that can be used as nutraceuticals. They want to generate new phenylpropanoids that have potential bioactivity. They have a PhD in biochemistry.

2. Walter works in molecule discovery at Amyris. They want to propose new sesquiterpenes with specific heats of combustion for use as biofuels. They hope to identify new sesquiterpenes with good diversity. They have an MS in natural product chemistry.

3. Jesse works is a researcher searching for new antibiotics. They want to propose new polyketides with predicted cytotoxicity that are synthetically accessible. They have a PhD in microbiology. 

4. Maliketh is an undergraduate student interested in natural products. They want to explore the generation of new natural products across different classes. They want to use the tool for explatory analysis. 

5. Malenia is a chemist at Antheia interested in the discovery of new therapeutics alkaloids. They want to propose new bioactive alkaloids for pain treatment based on chemical similarity to other alkaloids. They have a MS in biochemisry. 

6. Jose works in drug discovery at a pharma company. They want to explore natural products for use as
anticancer drugs. They want to generate molecules with predicted cytotoxcity. They are trained in small-molecule discovery. 

7. Lydia is a graduate student works in natural product biosynthesis. She wants to generate new natural products to evaluate a retrobiosynthesis tool she is developing. She is computationally proficient but does not have extensive lab experience.

8. Radagon is a consultant for pharma companies. They want to evaluate tools for drug discovery that can be useful for their clients. They want to do unconditional generation with drug-likeness properties to assess hit rate of tool in internal company validation metrics.

9. Morgott works at a biotechnology company interested in making cannabinoids. They want to propose new cannabinoids for preliminary molecule discovery. They want molecules that are classified as cannabinoids with decent diversity.

10. Kos is interested in identifying new receptor binder small molecules. They want to generate natural produc molecules with certain number of H-bond donors/acceptors and molecular weight. They are unfamiliar with natural products but experienced in biophysics.

11. Gus Fring is a researcher at Roche pharmacuticals. He wants to generate natural product analogues that they can use for drug discovery campaigns. He is experienced in the wet lab but does not have much computational expertise.

12. Marco Salamanca is a graduate student at the University of Wisconsin Madison. He is looking to discover inhibiors of natural enzymes by designing substrate analogues. He has some computational experience but is forced to rely heavily on AI to write software and parse documentation.

13. Skyler White is a principal scientist at the National Renewable Energy Institute. She is trying to develop a metabolic engineering toolkit to more easily synthesize natural-product like compounds. She is a metabolic engineering expert but is relatively new to computing.

14. Saul Goodman is a consultant for the national poison control center. He is looking for ways to better understand how natural compounds interact with human metabolism by investigating the effects of natural product analogues. He is proficient in microsoft office products.

15. Jessie Pinkman is a scientist at Genzyme Inc. He works in early stage development and is focused on developing methods to characterize the specificity of the enzymes they are developing. He is keenly interested in generating specific substrate analogues of natural enzymes.

16. Mike Ehrmantraut is an undergraduate at the University of Miami. He is curious about the use of generative artifical intellgience in chemistry and material science. He has no programming experience.

17. Walter White Junior is a physician-scientist at the Broad Institute. His resaearch is focused on developing new antibody-drug conjugates. The drugs are currently natural products and are difficult to synthesize. He is looking to screen new compounds that are synthetically accessible but have similar structural epitopes. He has minimal computational experience.

18. Tuco Salamanca is a scientist at the Food and Drug Administration who analyzing Investigational New Drug (IND) applications and is trying to evaluate if there might be drug candidates that might be easier to manufacture consistently at scale. He is well versed in modern computer-science techniques.

19. Hank Schrader is PI of a molecular simulation group at the University of Colorado Boulder and is interested finding synthetically accessible natural product mimics that his group can test and then validate in the wet lab. His programming experience is limited to FORTRAN.

20. Huell Babineaux is the CTO of a pharmacutical company and is looking to find new methods his company can use to generate natural-product like drug mimics. While he has a degree in comptuer science, it's been many years since he's written a program.

21. Dr. Kim is a medicinal chemist at a small pharmaceutical company. She wants to generate novel NP-like drug candidates that are both synthetically feasible and patentable. She needs high NP-likeness with low SA scores, and desires a tool that outputs ready-to-synthesize SMILES. Her chemistry expertise is deep but her coding skills are limited to basic Python scripts.

22. Mr.Song is an ML researcher at an AI lab. He wants to benchmark molecular generation methods using NP datasets. He needs standardized evaluation metrics, fair comparison protocols, and well-curated datasets. He has advanced ML skills but limited chemistry background.

23. Dr. Lee is a traditional medicine researcher. She wants to modernize herbal compound discovery by generating analogs of known ethnobotanical compounds. She needs organism source linking and culturally informed compound selection. She is a domain expert with beginner-level coding skills.

24. Mr.Choi is a patent attorney at a pharmaceutical firm. He wants to assess the novelty of generated molecules against existing databases. He needs a uniqueness checking tool with clear reports and database cross-referencing. He has no technical background.

25. Dr. Park is an antibiotic discovery researcher. She wants to generate antimicrobial NP candidates to address drug-resistant bacteria. She needs activity-conditioned generation. She has intermediate ML skills and deep microbiology expertise.

26. Mr. Norris is an undergraduate student. He wants to learn molecular generation for his capstone project. He needs easy setup, clear documentation. He is a beginner in both ML and chemistry.

27. Dr. Hamilton is a clinical pharmacologist. He wants to optimize NP leads for better oral bioavailability. He needs QED optimization and property prediction. He is a domain expert with basic scripting ability.

28. Dr. Senna is a bioinformatics postdoc. He wants to connect generated NPs to biosynthetic gene clusters for metabolic engineering. He needs multi-modal output linking structures to BGC data. He has advanced coding skills but basic chemistry knowledge.

29. Mr. Schumacher manages a contract research organization. He wants to offer NP generation as a service to pharmaceutical clients. He needs batch processing, automated report generation, and high reliability. He has an advanced engineering background.

30. Mr. Verstappen is an open-source community contributor. He wants to extend the model with new datasets and generation methods. He needs modular codebase, clear contribution guidelines, and comprehensive API documentation. He has advanced programming skills and growing chemistry interest.
